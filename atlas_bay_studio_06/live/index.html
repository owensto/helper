The revised fishing boat has a rebuilt hull and cockpit, integrated rigid top, wraparound windshield and matched seating. Use Compare Studio 04 to compare geometry from the same camera.

The naval models are original, non-operational visual designs. No combat, stealth, pressure, propulsion or seaworthiness rating is established. Their modeled dimensions are visual planning envelopes.

ATLAS-D preserves the prior yacht design and its speculative upper-shell transformation. The new Bay 25 fishing tender replaces the utility tender. Its rigid T-top, outboard and secured fishing gear remain present during recovery.

The garage result is a selected mesh/cylindrical-barrel containment screen—not a qualified recovery system. Aperture and pressure-door sweeps, freeboard, loads, supports, tolerances, waves, stability and safe diving remain unresolved.

Cutaway transparencies and hidden roof sections are inspection aids, not physical transformations. Playback is prescribed visual choreography, not real operational timing.

ATLAS BAY 25 / DESIGN STUDY

Shallow water.
Without a made-up draft rating.

The default keeps the approved hull. A separate unvalidated shallow-aft-V option keeps the same topsides, deck and T-top. These calculations compare hull volume at even keel, not measured boat performance.

Loading calculation…
Not a payload or operating-depth rating. Total mass includes hull, top, glass, engine, rigging, batteries, fuel, people, tackle, ice and livewell water. Trim, stability, structure and actual all-up mass are not solved. Engine/propeller, trim tabs, strakes, transducers and trolling motor are separate clearance limits. Floating in 12 inches does not prove safe idling, acceleration or running in 12 inches.

What the alternative changes: the aft bottom V becomes shallower while the chines, topsides and accommodation stay fixed. No engine is removed, hidden or scaled. This is not a tunnel-hull design or a qualified modification.
