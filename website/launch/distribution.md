# First distribution sprint

## The offer

“Booked a fishing trip? Build a free checklist based on where you're going, what you already own, and what your guide provides.”

Do not lead with “the world's largest fishing atlas” or promise comprehensive worldwide coverage in this first release.

## Three experiments, not a giant launch

### 1. Operator distribution

Select 10 relevant guides or lodges across the supported destinations. These are proposed outreach targets, not a list of already recruited partners. Research each operator before messaging. Create an example preparation list, but never mark a provision confirmed on their behalf.

Send the personal introduction from the app's sharing kit. Offer to help turn their actual equipment list into a client checklist. First success: an operator sends the link to real booked customers. Measure repeat sends and useful customer feedback, not just an initial reply.

### 2. First-person trip content

Use original footage and observations from an actual fishing trip. A 20–40 second demo can show the problem: an already-booked trip, too many gear tabs, and uncertainty about what is supplied. Show the checklist removing owned items. Finish with “Build your free trip checklist.”

Three hooks are included in the app. Publish factual, firsthand observations, and link to the relevant checklist. No content has been posted by this build.

### 3. Search-oriented guides

The first six articles are already included. Review them against real user questions rather than commissioning dozens of generic “best gear” pages. Add local detail and exact reviewed products where it improves the answer. Connect actual search performance after publication.

## Measure one clear funnel

Source → article or planner → updated checklist → shared/exported plan → retailer or operator click → actual earnings/booking outcome when integrations exist.

A saved checklist is product engagement. It is not revenue. An operator inquiry is not a booking. A retailer click is not a purchase. No advertising has been purchased or campaign launched.

## First feedback questions

Did the list prevent a missing item? Did it prevent an unnecessary purchase? Did anyone assume a guide provided something that it did not? Would the operator send it again?

Keep the build small while answering those questions. Add exact product catalog depth, additional destinations and native bookings in response to demonstrated demand.
