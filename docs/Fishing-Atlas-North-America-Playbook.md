# Fishing Atlas — North America production playbook

**Version:** 13 September 2026. **Scope:** 357 U.S. experience briefs, 100 Canada briefs, 50 Mexico briefs. **First release queue:** two experiences per U.S. state, 100 total. **Approach:** build and distribute in small regional batches; no large planning-budget or hiring exercise.


This is a production plan and structured backlog, not a claim that 507 fishing guides have been researched, independently vetted or published. Candidate species, access modes and proposed titles remain subject to source verification. The existing website has not been modified or deployed by this deliverable. Existing guides and useful international records are retained, not discarded.


## 1. Definition of success

Make a visitor ready for a specific fishing experience: identify a suitable fishery and method, understand timing and access, distinguish equipment owned or supplied from items missing, and take a useful shopping or booking action. A page that merely changes the place name does not meet this goal. The product must be useful even when the visitor buys nothing or booked the guide elsewhere.


The unit of production is a **fishing opportunity**, not a keyword permutation: a named water or departure area, a supported target/method, a practical access mode, a qualified seasonal context and an independently useful preparation decision. Several opportunities can share one waterbody; this is not a count of 507 independent lakes or rivers.

## 3. What each published trip guide must contain

Open with who the experience is for, what kind of fishing is actually supported, the key decision before booking or buying, and the most important uncertainty. Do not bury the practical answer beneath an introductory travel essay.

| Section | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| Quick decision | Target, environment, suitable experience level, access type and what must be confirmed. |
| Choose the trip | Compare meaningful local variants: bank versus boat, short versus long run, DIY versus guided, one species versus another. |
| When and why | A source-supported seasonal matrix, variables that change the answer, and separate recent observations. Unknown months stay unknown. |
| Where and access | Named reach or port, arrival/meeting logistics, launch/trail/shuttle considerations and an alternative when access changes. |
| How to fish | Explain the selected method and local presentation decisions; distinguish alternate methods instead of recommending incompatible gear. |
| Complete preparation | Equipment requirements, clothing for the actual itinerary, quantities, luggage limits, owned gear and operator-provided gear. |
| Shop missing items | Reviewed exact variants; suitable budget/recommended/premium choices; rationale, compatibility and when to skip the purchase. |
| Book or arrange | Actual guide matching or honest inquiry flow; confirmed inclusions, not invented availability or prices. |
| Local mistakes and questions | Resolve the row-specific research question plus at least two additional meaningful local decisions. |
| Sources and revision | Claim-level sources, geographic scope, evidence dates, reviewer identity/scope and remaining uncertainty. |


Article length is determined by the decisions that must be explained. Do not optimize to a word count. A generic long article is not better than a useful short one; a complex offshore or expedition trip may need a much larger supporting guide.

## 5. Gear and affiliate shopping

Keep **requirement → exact product → retailer offer** as separate records. The requirement describes what the fishing method and access need. The product record contains the exact model/variant, specifications, compatible components, limitations and recommendation basis. The offer contains retailer, marketplace, product identifier, affiliate tracking and permitted availability/price data.


The customer-facing planner distinguishes already owned, confirmed operator-provided, missing, optional, rent locally and confirm before purchase. Unknown supplied equipment does not become a shopping recommendation by default. A guided offshore boat should not sell the visitor a duplicate fighting outfit; a hike-in alpine day should not inherit a chest-wader shopping list.


At launch, curate exact relevant product links rather than calling category searches “vetted products.” For each important requirement provide one preferred fit, a lower-cost alternative where suitable and a premium alternative only with a defensible benefit. Do not force three products when only one is suitable or none needs buying. Use the Amazon Creators API when eligible and available; API access is a dependency, not a reason to block a useful curated checklist. Source: https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/introduction .


**A product-selection record must include:** model and variant; role in the setup; target method; size/rating criteria; compatible line/leader/terminal components where relevant; why recommended; why not recommended; ownership/provision exclusions; source specifications; date checked; reviewer; alternative; exact retailer identifier. No exact gear models have been vetted in this planning package.

## 6. Research and quality workflow

The state directory is a route to primary sources, not a replacement for water-specific evidence. Texas Lake Finder, for example, links access information and fishing tips for more than 150 lakes. Build on this kind of structured local material, then add practical operator/shop corroboration and original preparation decisions. Sources: https://www.fishwildlife.org/landing/membership/member-list ; https://tpwd.texas.gov/fishboat/fish/recreational/lakes/ .


For each assertion, keep claim text, source URL, source type, observed/effective date, geographic scope, evidence excerpt or internal note, and confidence/limitations. Do not reuse “reviewed today” to imply the underlying event or seasonal observation occurred today. Full source-page reading is required before a claim is marked verified; snippets remain discovery leads.

| 03 | Local check | Local reviewer / operator | Confirm practical method, access, what visitors miss and what the operator provides | Corrections and recorded review scope |
| 04 | Gear plan | Gear editor | Resolve method/access; select requirements; check exact products and compatible alternatives | Reusable requirement set and reviewed retailer offers |

No candidate has been assigned a fabricated completed quality score. Record actual local review before using an expert-review badge. Lower-risk established facts can be source-checked; location-specific recommendations still need practical corroboration.

For the first active region, a reasonable working experiment is five personally selected operator or shop conversations—not a large scraped outreach blast. Show a preparation sample specific to their trips. The goal is repeated use in confirmations, not merely a directory signup.


Suggested opening: “We built a preparation page for this trip. Customers mark what they own, see what you provide, and get only the missing items. Which parts would you change before sending it to your customers?” Incorporate their corrections before presenting them as partners.
