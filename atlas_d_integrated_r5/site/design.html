ATLAS D / 05

Integrated exterior, restrained conversion.

No demonstrated safe diving capability. This is an arrangement and kinematic study. It is not pressure-vessel engineering, a dynamic simulation, a construction design, or a rated submersible. The illustrated descent is only a camera/model sequence.

What changed

The detached folding pavilions have been removed. A stepped but continuous, tapered deckhouse, recessed glazing and a swept fixed crown now form the surface silhouette. The lower cylindrical pressure-core arrangement is retained as an inspection reference. No occupied enclosure shrinks or folds.

What remains unresolved

The fixed upper deckhouses are still surface-use exterior architecture in this study, outside the lower proposed pressure enclosure. Giving them enclosed yacht glazing does not make them safe dry rooms underwater. Accommodation, seawater-compatible finishes, venting, trapped-air paths, doors, service isolation and drainage need a complete arrangement redesign before any functional claim.

The pressure capsule, energy and life-support shapes carried from R3 are layout proxies, not qualified components. Wall thickness, material grade, penetrations, manufacture, fatigue, buckling, mass budget, centers of gravity/buoyancy, stability, drag, propulsion power, endurance and independent recovery have not been established.

This pass concentrates on coherent external form and identifiable stowage. Passing browser tests demonstrates correct geometry loading and playback; it does not prove mechanism loads, global non-interference or pressure integrity.

Viewing the actual geometry

Convert fittings stops above water. Before / Stowed toggles matching-camera views. The timeline allows intermediate inspection. Stowage inspection deliberately ghosts the fixed roof as a diagnostic overlay; the normal conversion never fades the hull or pressure core.
