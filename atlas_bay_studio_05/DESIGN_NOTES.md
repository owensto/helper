# ATLAS Bay 25 — Studio 05 / fishing & shallow-water study

## Baseline decisions
One outboard retained. Six T-top fishing-rod holders: four original rear holders plus one additional supported, lined tube on each outer aft corner. No extra rods are silently added to an operational load. The rigid top, approved hull, console and seats are retained in the default model. Soft rod rests, a flush 50 cm measuring scale, tackle-tool pocket and electric trolling-motor propeller are added. These are visual hardware details, not qualified installations.

The separate shallow-bottom option is an **unvalidated alternative**. It reduces aft-V depth while retaining the existing chines, topsides, furnishings and engine. It is not an animated hull morph and does not change the approved default. It is not a tunnel hull. The lower-unit position/clearance has not been optimized for this altered bottom.

## Draft requirement
Investigate **less than 12 inches of static hull draft at a specifically declared fishing load**, with the engine raised. Aim for margin rather than exactly 12 inches. This is not permission to operate in water of that depth. The motor/propeller, trim tabs, transducers, strakes and trolling motor are separately limiting appendages. Do not equate hull draft, idle depth, planing depth or acceleration clearance.

The draft panel does not move the model's waterline to imply a rating. It shows cross-section calculations independently of the presentation animation. Actual all-up mass, longitudinal/vertical CG, trim, stability, freeboard and construction remain unknown.

## Calculation method and limits
The section functions are the same PCHIP station functions that generated the approved Studio 03/04 hull. Cross-sectional submerged area is integrated over 1,601 longitudinal stations at even keel, using seawater density 1,025 kg/m³ or freshwater 1,000 kg/m³ as declared inputs. Topsides use the existing curved station formula; the alternative changes only the bottom below the chines.

A result in kg is the **equivalent total displacement mass** at a hypothetical immersion. It includes everything aboard and is not extra payload or a certified weight limit. The actual mass register must include structure, top, glass, motor, rigging, fuel, batteries, crew/passengers, livewell water, ice and tackle. No material thickness, structure weight or actual equipment weight is invented from a decorative mesh.

At 11.9 inches and even keel, the original hull envelope corresponds to approximately 1.11 tonnes total in seawater and the alternate envelope to approximately 1.92 tonnes. These numbers show why hull design and the mass/trim budget matter; they do NOT demonstrate that an equipped Atlas floats at 11.9 inches. Neither the trim equilibrium nor the displacement contributed by immersed accessories is solved. A structurally lighter boat cannot be assumed merely by assigning a different material name.

## Engine and layout recommendation
Continue investigating a single 250–300 hp class installation, with final power based on structural rating and a selected engine/propeller installation. No specific horsepower, speed, mounting height or safe jackplate setting is approved by this model. A second outboard adds another engine and installation to package and support; redundant propulsion can be useful for offshore missions, but it is not the baseline for this shallow-fishing tender.

A jackplate can change lower-unit clearance; it does not remove engine mass or by itself establish reduced static hull draft. Keep the casting deck open. Place batteries and fuel only after the CG/trim study; do not simply move all weight forward. Review a modest working fuel load, one appropriately sized livewell, lightweight qualified top construction and removable comfort cushions rather than adding a tower and heavy amenities.
