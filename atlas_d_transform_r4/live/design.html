Revision 04 — a visible conversion

Design change, not an animation trick: the previous upper enclosed rooms and roof decks are replaced in this variant by two OPEN leisure canopies over one fixed terrace. Those occupied volumes do not collapse or shrink during playback; they are no longer part of this design.

What moves

Daybed backs fold flat. The radar folds onto the forward roof. Fixed-length scissor links lower the sculpted canopies. Side terraces rotate into exterior casing panels. Linked rigid fairing slats roll from a modeled cassette over the aft pool terrace. Roofs, mechanism bars and exterior panels retain their dimensions.

The forward canopy roof drops 5.16 m. This is non-pressure structure: nobody may remain in its closure envelope. Scissor guides, pivots and mounting locations illustrate paths, but hinge loads, hydraulics, tolerances, collision/clearance verification and structural redundancy are unresolved.

What stays fixed

The main hull meshes and lower candidate pressure capsule from R3 are unchanged. The capsule remains a geometric arrangement without pressure-shell thickness, verified viewports, qualified hatches or a depth rating. Outer covers are not pressure seals.

How to see the difference

Transform only runs the conversion and holds at the surface. Before / Ready alternates configurations from the same profile view. Canopies and Stern show close-ups. Full sequence continues through the inherited illustrative dive.

Scientific boundary

Separating a fixed pressure hull from floodable outer casing is established submarine architecture. This does not validate these moving canopies. The 40 m descent is prescribed kinematics, not a buoyancy/dynamics simulation or allowed depth. Pressure, mass, stability, energy, fatigue, sealing, recovery and sea-state operation remain unverified.

Source: Canadian submarine principles manual — pressure hull, casing and ballast. This source does not review or endorse ATLAS D.
