ATLAS DESIGN LAB / REVISION 03

Design the pressure boundary.
Then animate the dive.

A more scientifically grounded arrangement—not a certified submersible.

This vessel has no demonstrated safe operating depth. The 40 m descent in the viewer is a visualization setting, not a rating. The animation does not prove water-tightness, collapse resistance, stability, buoyancy, endurance or emergency recovery.

1. The architecture

The proposed dry enclosure is a fixed cylindrical pressure capsule with hemispherical ends inside the yacht hull. The outer yacht form is a fairing around it. Upper pavilions are designated floodable, surface-only spaces. They must be emptied of people before diving and built from suitable flood-compatible systems. Conventional interior finishes and ordinary enclosed yacht rooms cannot simply be left underwater.

This is a deliberate tradeoff. Keeping all of the original upper accommodation dry would require additional pressure-resistant structures, a different accommodation layout and a revised weight/stability study. Closing decorative shutters would not solve that.

2. What the shape should do

The occupied pressure vessel should not shrink, unfold or change shape during a dive. Its full-strength geometry exists in both modes. Surface fittings may stow, covers may close and control surfaces may deploy. A major yacht-to-torpedo morph would demand explicit storage spaces and joints that this concept does not have.

Revision 03 retains the same hull and deck architecture. It adds a pressure-boundary cutaway, cylindrical core, indicative stiffening rings, candidate small viewports, access closures, dual air-service isolation, floodable-space visualization, main ballast, trim tanks, electric-propulsor fairings, foil-shaped foreplanes, an air-bank arrangement and releasable weights. The geometry is not a hydrodynamic optimization.

4. What is calculated

Hydrostatic pressure uses Δp = ρgh, with assumed seawater density ρ = 1,025 kg/m³ and gravity g = 9.80665 m/s². The dry cabin is assumed to remain at 1.01325 bar absolute. Displayed external differential pressure is evaluated at the model datum. The inspector separately evaluates the deepest point of the proposed pressure capsule, 3 m below that datum.

These are load calculations, not allowable-pressure calculations. They say what the structure must resist—not whether it can resist it.

The idealized capsule gross volume is approximately 2,150 m³. The eight main-ballast proxies total 409.6 m³ before deductions for structure and equipment. Neither is a mass budget or usable accommodation figure. No reserve-buoyancy percentage, depth rating or stability margin is claimed.

5. Functional demonstrations

Hatch fault: at the surface, an obstructed candidate hatch remains open and the viewer stops progress before flooding and descent. At depth, a fault indication pauses the scene without opening a pressure hatch. This is demonstrative logic—not safety-rated software.

Trim: forward and aft water fractions change by equal and opposite amounts. The water transfer is conserved in the schematic; the actual pitch response is not solved.

Recovery: releasable weights fall away while the vessel rises along a prescribed path and pressure hatches remain closed. No weight mass, emergency-gas inventory or recovered freeboard is established.

6. Engineering gates before human use

Arrangement and weight: close the mass and displaced-volume budgets, including all floodable spaces, liquids, payload and equipment. Establish centers of gravity and buoyancy, surface stability, submerged stability and transition/damage cases. The present waterline is retained from the visual model, not from a solved equilibrium.

Pressure structure: select materials, shell thickness, stiffening, junctions, seats and penetrations under an applicable class design basis. Analyze external-pressure buckling with fabrication imperfections, fatigue/cyclic service, opening reinforcements and failure scenarios. A closed triangle mesh is not a qualified pressure vessel.

Systems: size propulsion, batteries, thermal management, isolation, atmosphere control, fire protection, reserve oxygen, carbon-dioxide removal and independent emergency energy. Demonstrate ascent/recovery capability after credible failures and establish a rescue/support concept.

Validation: independent specialist review, manufacturing traceability, nondestructive examination, hydrostatic and cyclic tests, unmanned trials, progressive sea trials and certification must precede any claim of safe occupied diving.
