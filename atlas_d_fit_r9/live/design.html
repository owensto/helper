ATLAS D / Measured fit revision 09

No demonstrated safe diving depth. This is a geometric arrangement and animation study. It does not establish pressure resistance, watertightness, structural capacity, habitability, stability, endurance, or emergency recovery. The prescribed descent is not a dynamic simulation.

1. The exterior stays recognizable

The original layered yacht, bow helipad and tender pair remain. The existing nested-shell art-study motion is retained for comparison; its structural support, full packing clearance and sea-going accommodation are not solved. All permanent room reservations belong to a separate fixed dry-core candidate, not to the moving upper shells.

3. The handling sequence now respects bore entry

The empty carrier first extends from its stored elevation. Only after clearing the aft opening does it lower to the capture elevation. A tender aligns behind the stern, the carrier raises it outside the opening, and both translate straight along the garage axis. The outer styling doors close after the second insertion. Both craft remain aboard during the illustrative descent; neither tender is rescaled or replaced.

Each path is screened at 241 temporal positions against the idealized circular barrel. Fixed transverse geometry makes the straight-insertion portion particularly inspectable. The check excludes the free approach, surrounding outer aperture, pressure-closure sweep, waves, vessel motion, deflection, manufacturing tolerances, personnel access and complete system collisions. It is not a certified working-clearance result.

4. Fixed accommodation candidate

The 8.6 m diameter, 50.6 m overall dry-envelope candidate contains two fixed deck reservations, each approximately 41.0 × 6.1 m. Full-height room boxes are screened against a 3.9 m planning radius at 2.2 m clear height. The 0.40 m radial allowance is for structure, frames and services together; it is not an engineered shell thickness. Five two-person guest-suite reservations, a two-person owner suite and twelve twin-bunk crew cabins represent a target of 12 guests and 24 crew, not a habitability or escape approval.

5. What remains unresolved

Recovery opening: its candidate lower edge is only 0.22 m above the unsolved visual waterline. Wave response, surface equilibrium, recovery freeboard and any lock arrangement must be resolved before opening-at-sea capability can be claimed.

Garage aperture and pressure closures: the exterior styling is not yet redrawn around qualified circular pressure passages. A ring or closed digital mesh does not provide a certified pressure door.

Engineering: carrier loads, mechanisms, contact pads, supporting structure, stiffness, fatigue, emergency access, full swept clearances, main-hull integration, mass, centers of gravity/buoyancy, surface and submerged stability, power, life support and independent recovery remain unqualified.

6. Buoyancy calculator: explicitly hypothetical

The lab calculates fully immersed gross displacement of the main and two garage candidate envelopes at assumed seawater density 1,025 kg/m³. It gives no enclosed-volume credit to freely flooding fairings. The all-up mass is unknown: the optional 3,300 t example is user-adjustable hypothetical input, not an estimate. Subtracting a garage gross outer volume is an upper-bound sensitivity only, not an exact flooded-void or asymmetric damage-stability solution. Positive net buoyancy in that example is not evidence of a safe ascent.

7. Next engineering gates

Resolve the aft recovery opening and actual pressure-closure geometry; size carrier loads and allow adequate clearances; close a component-level mass/displacement register; assess centers and stability through transition and failure cases; then establish structural, systems and test requirements with appropriate specialists. The revised exterior must follow those results rather than hiding conflicts with animation.
