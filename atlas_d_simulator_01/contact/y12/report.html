A little faster, without a faster clock

ATLAS-D retains its 2,400,000 kg concept mass, source hull and physical-size footprint. Higher forward thrust and a 1.8-second propulsion response replace Y11’s 14-knot tuning. Simulation still consumes the ordinary fixed 1/60-second steps. The ship keeps momentum and develops sideslip; rudder travel is rate-limited. A larger vessel still needs room to stop.

What is modeled

Surge, sway and yaw force/inertia equations, energy-consistent Coriolis coupling, linear and quadratic resistance, 16-strip crossflow, rudder side force with simplified propwash, and separately damped heave/roll/pitch. Each vessel has its own length, beam, draft, inertia and control response. The original ATLAS-D animation closes its exterior; its canned descent track is never used as dynamics.

The 39-point conservative hull footprint checks the same bottom used for the visible scene. Unknown coverage is unavailable, not assumed deep. ATLAS-D must secure before descending. ATLAS-N and SUMARS start in submarine configuration; ATLAS-W cannot dive. Selecting another vessel removes the previous owner and geometry, starts at the safe origin and leaves propulsion off.

What is not modeled or certified

No full six-degree-of-freedom hydrodynamics, real measured resistance curves, pressure-hull design, dynamic flooding, swept collision guarantees, certified occupancy, combat systems, or multiplayer. Depth hold is a bounded simplified controller, not realistic ballast engineering. Longer stopping or higher speed alone is not evidence of real-world accuracy.

Reproduction

Run node atlas_d_simulator_01/contact/y12/test-fleet.cjs from the pinned checkout. All maneuvers use calm synthetic 200 m water, 1/60 s steps; coast/reverse begin after 720 s full-forward equilibration. No browser frame-rate or performance claim follows from these deterministic tests.

Original source assets are unchanged. SUMARS is a newly authored two-seat visual concept; no pressure or passenger-safety rating. Isolated single-player fleet release, not CORE’s integrated Beaufort or multiplayer release.
