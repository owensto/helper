This is an authored offshore simulation, not a real navigation chart. Coefficients, water depths, wind/current presets and vertical-control assumptions are inspectable. They are not calibrated to real ATLAS sea trials, CFD, pressure qualification, or passenger safety.

Navigation you can actually use

Tap the north-up chart to lay a route of up to eight waypoints. It shows your track, target bearing, remaining range, signed course error and ETA only when your actual velocity is closing on the target. Heading and course over ground are separate; speed through water is not confused with speed over ground. Routes and the 30-second constant-motion projection are checked for low/unknown water across the vessel’s own conservative footprint. These are sampled advisory checks, not a guarantee against grounding, an autopilot or route optimization.

Physical changes

Horizontal forces now act on current-relative velocity, with the rotating-body current terms included. Wind acts through relative air velocity and authored projected areas; submerged wind loads diminish. Thrust and rudder actuators keep their finite response. Sea response samples a 21-point hull-scale wave plane from the same calculation used to render the waves.

Submarine vertical motion now integrates a bounded control force through effective mass, drag and actuator lag. Instruments show actual vertical rate and control force. This is a force-limited depth controller—not detailed ballast tanks, flooding, pressure-hull engineering, or complete six-degree-of-freedom coupling.

A separate air-resistance term reduces still-water speed slightly despite unchanged thrust; that difference is reported rather than called exact agreement with Y12. These figures are simulation outputs, not vessel specifications.

Graphics changes

Distinct paint, naval coating, rubber, teak, upholstery, metal and acrylic optics; a detailed SUMARS canopy flange and thruster guards; coherent near-field water, underwater color loss, path-following wake ribbons, outdoor reflections, wet surface roughness and camera fitting from visible geometry. Shared material instances reduce duplicate setup. Planar reflections are budgeted and off in automatic software-renderer mode. Neither those budgets nor fewer duplicate materials establish a measured frame-rate improvement.

Reproduce the checks

From this directory, run node test-navigation.cjs, node test-simulation.cjs, and node test-realism.cjs. The source bundle retains raw output and any actual browser evidence separately. Code/Three.js geometry tests are not shader/GPU or physical-device certification.

Model references

The uniform-current relative-velocity formulation is informed by Fossen’s Marine Craft Model. Rendering uses Three.js physical materials. These references do not validate the authored numerical coefficients.
