What changed for all four

The visible water and vessel wave sampling now use the same six-component gravity-wave field. Frequencies use the finite-depth dispersion equation at an explicitly assumed 78 m reference depth. A dense local mesh follows those heights instead of rendering a flat surface between very widely spaced vertices.

The existing damped hull-response model samples a weighted 27-point waterplane fit. It does not pretend a 125 m warship and a 6.4 m pod respond identically. Mass, propulsive force, steering and calm-water maneuvering remain unchanged from Y12; ATLAS-D still equilibrates near 18 knots.

Water reflects the actual scene at an approximate mean-sea-level mirror plane. Outdoor lighting, surface roughness, paint/teak/rubber distinctions and restrained wet bands improve material cues. Wake geometry follows retained owner positions, rather than turning a straight trail instantly with the bow. All visual animation uses the existing owner time.

SUMARS: still Kayley’s baby-blue pod

New collar hardware, rounded ivory bucket seats, headrests, armrests, foot supports, grab handles, actual-state cockpit readouts and guarded propulsors replace the previous broad primitive presentation. The transparent canopy uses a transmissive physical material. This detail is visual design work, not a validated submersible construction plan.

Known approximations

Wave amplitudes/directions are authored; reference depth is uniform, not surveyed or shoaling-aware. The waterplane shape and restoring periods are not calibrated hydrostatics. Planar reflections, foam and underwater attenuation are optical approximations. Depth control remains simplified. Original R7 yacht conversion is still conceptual. No new pressure-hull, passenger safety, source-triangle swept collision, human fit or integrated release acceptance.
